# AI Video Studio --- Video 01

## Stage 1: Concept

### Project

**Video Title:** *The Little Lantern*\
**Project ID:** `01_THE_LITTLE_LANTERN`\
**Production Stage:** 01 --- Concept\
**Target Runtime:** Approximately 4 minutes\
**Format:** Cinematic animated short story\
**Audience:** Family / children, with emotional appeal for adults\
**Learning Level:** Beginner-friendly production using a professional,
repeatable workflow

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Core Idea

### Premise

In a quiet mountain village, every home carries a beautiful lantern. But
one tiny lantern named **Lumi** believes her light is too small to
matter.

When a violent storm extinguishes the village's great beacon and a young
girl becomes lost in the forest, Lumi discovers that even the smallest
light can show someone the way home.

### Theme

> **You don't have to be the brightest to make a difference.**

### Emotional Goal

The audience should begin by feeling sympathy for Lumi's insecurity,
experience tension when the storm arrives and the girl becomes lost, and
finish with warmth and hope when Lumi discovers her value.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Creative Direction

### Visual Direction

Storybook-inspired cinematic animation featuring:

-   Warm, inviting village lighting
-   Expressive characters
-   Rich natural environments
-   Strong contrast between warm lantern light and cool night
    environments
-   Cinematic composition and depth
-   Consistent character design across every scene
-   Atmospheric weather and lighting
-   A warm, emotionally satisfying ending

### Story Tone

-   Gentle
-   Magical
-   Emotional
-   Adventurous
-   Hopeful
-   Family-friendly

### Production Goal

The finished video should feel like one coherent animated short film
rather than a sequence of unrelated AI-generated images.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Why This Story Is Video 01

*The Little Lantern* is designed to teach the complete AI-video
production workflow.

The story includes:

-   A recurring hero character
-   A human character
-   Multiple environments
-   Day and night scenes
-   Weather
-   Emotional close-ups
-   Action
-   Lighting changes
-   A cinematic climax
-   A clear emotional resolution

These elements will require us to learn **character consistency,
environmental consistency, visual continuity, shot design, animation,
narration, sound design, editing, and publishing**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. AI Video Studio Production Workflow

Every video in the studio will follow the same core pipeline:

**Idea → Story → Script → Character Bible → Storyboard → Scene List →
Image Prompts → Consistent AI Images → Image-to-Video / Animation →
Voiceover → Music → Sound Effects → Editing → Captions → Thumbnail →
Export → Publishing**

We will improve the workflow as we learn, but changes should be
deliberate and documented so future productions remain repeatable.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Studio Rules Established in Stage 1

### Rule 1 --- Do Not Generate Final Scenes Too Early

We will not jump directly from a story idea to random scene generation.

Generating prompts such as:

> "Girl walking through magical forest with lantern"

may produce an attractive image, but it does not establish the
character, clothing, lantern design, environment, lighting, or visual
style required for a consistent film.

### Rule 2 --- Lock the Foundations First

Our consistency sequence is:

**Story → Characters → Visual Language → Master Reference Images →
Shots**

Only after these foundations are approved will we generate production
scene images.

### Rule 3 --- Use Quality Gates

Each production stage must pass a quality checkpoint before moving to
the next stage.

Rejected assets should be retained separately when useful so we can
learn why they failed.

### Rule 4 --- Keep Production Assets Organised

Files should use predictable names and live in the correct production
folder.

This will make the project easier to edit manually and easier to
automate later with Codex or other tools.

### Rule 5 --- Learn First, Automate Second

Video 01 will teach the workflow manually.

After we understand which tasks are repetitive and stable, we can
progressively automate them using Codex, scripts, FFmpeg, manifests,
validation tools, and other production utilities.

## 7. Studio Automation Strategy

The studio will develop along two parallel tracks.

### Track A --- Video Production

Learn how to create high-quality AI-assisted story videos from concept
through publishing.

### Track B --- Studio Automation

Gradually use Codex and other development tools to automate repetitive
production tasks such as:

-   Creating project folders
-   Generating scene manifests
-   Managing filenames
-   Tracking scene status
-   Checking missing assets
-   Maintaining prompt versions
-   Preparing subtitle files
-   Generating FFmpeg commands
-   Preparing editing packages
-   Validating final exports

Automation should only be introduced after the underlying production
process is understood.
