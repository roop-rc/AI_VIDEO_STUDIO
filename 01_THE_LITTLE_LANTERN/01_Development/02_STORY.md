# The Little Lantern

High among the mountains stood a little village called Bellwood.

Every evening, just as the sun disappeared behind the snowy peaks, the
villagers lit hundreds of lanterns. Golden lights appeared in windows,
beside doorways and along the winding stone streets.

And high above them all, at the top of an old stone tower, burned the
Great Lantern.

Its enormous flame could be seen from far across the valley.

But in a tiny workshop at the edge of the village lived a very different
lantern.

Her name was Lumi.

Lumi was small enough to fit into a child's hands. Her brass frame was
scratched, one side was slightly crooked, and the flame inside her was
no bigger than a firefly.

Every night, Lumi watched the other lanterns shine.

Some hung proudly outside the grandest houses.

Some illuminated the busy village square.

And the Great Lantern shone brighter than all of them.

Lumi looked at her tiny flame.

"What good is a light this small?" she wondered.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

One afternoon, a girl named Maya came into the workshop.

Maya loved exploring the hills and forests surrounding Bellwood.

She noticed Lumi sitting quietly on a shelf.

"This one," Maya said.

The shopkeeper looked surprised.

"There are brighter lanterns."

But Maya smiled and picked Lumi up carefully.

"I like this one."

For the first time in a long while, Lumi's little flame flickered
brightly.

That evening, Maya carried Lumi along a forest path while collecting
wildflowers.

But beyond the mountains, dark clouds were gathering.

The wind began to howl.

Rain crashed through the trees.

Maya turned toward home, but the familiar path had disappeared beneath
fallen branches and swirling leaves.

Then---

**WHOOSH!**

Across the valley, the Great Lantern went dark.

One by one, the village lights vanished behind the storm.

Maya stopped.

She could no longer see Bellwood.

For the first time, she was afraid.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

The wind battered Lumi's little flame.

It bent left.

It bent right.

It almost disappeared.

Lumi remembered the enormous flame of the Great Lantern.

"I'm too small," she thought.

But Maya held her close.

"Please don't go out."

Something changed inside Lumi.

She could not light the whole forest.

She could not light the whole mountain.

But perhaps she didn't need to.

Lumi pushed her little flame as brightly as she could.

A warm circle of light appeared around Maya's feet.

And inside that tiny circle was something they had not noticed before.

A stone.

Carved into it was an arrow pointing toward Bellwood.

Maya followed it.

Farther along, Lumi illuminated another marker.

Then another.

One tiny pool of light at a time, they moved through the darkness.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Back in the village, people gathered anxiously beneath the dark tower.

"Maya is still out there!" someone cried.

The villagers stared toward the enormous black forest.

There was nothing to see.

Then an old woman pointed toward the trees.

"Look!"

Far away, between the trunks, something flickered.

A tiny golden light.

It disappeared.

Then appeared again.

"There!"

The villagers began lighting their lanterns.

One lantern became ten.

Ten became fifty.

Soon hundreds of villagers stood along the edge of Bellwood, holding
their lights high.

Together they created a glowing path home.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Through the rain, Maya saw something golden between the trees.

"Home!"

She ran toward the lights.

When she finally reached the village, everyone rushed to meet her.

Maya was soaked and muddy.

Lumi's brass frame was covered in scratches.

And her tiny flame barely flickered.

But it was still burning.

Above the village, the Great Lantern eventually came back to life.

Once again its powerful flame illuminated the valley.

Lumi looked up at it.

Then she looked at the hundreds of smaller lanterns surrounding Maya.

For the first time, Lumi did not wish that she were brighter.

Because now she understood.

A light doesn't have to illuminate the whole world.

Sometimes...

it only needs to illuminate the next step.

And from that night onward, whenever someone in Bellwood felt too small
to matter, they remembered the little lantern whose tiny light had shown
the way home.

**The End**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Story Intent

### Central Theme

**You don't have to be the brightest to make a difference.**

### Emotional Arc

Lumi begins the story believing that her small light makes her
unimportant. Through helping Maya navigate the forest one step at a
time, she discovers that usefulness is not determined by size or power.

### Visual Motif

The climax should progressively develop the motif:

**Darkness → tiny circle of light → another circle → distant light →
many lights → path of light**

This motif should be preserved during screenplay, storyboard,
image-generation and editing stages.

### Important Story Rule

Lumi must **not** solve the climax by suddenly becoming extraordinarily
bright or magically powerful.

Her light remains small.

Its importance comes from illuminating the next useful thing, while the
combined lights of the villagers ultimately create the path home.
